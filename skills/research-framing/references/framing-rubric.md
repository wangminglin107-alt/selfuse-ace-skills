# Research-framing rubric

## Pass conditions

The brief distinguishes an observable or proposed phenomenon from the scholarly problem created
by it. It names both the unit and level of analysis, provides at least one working construct and
research question, and states a contribution as a hypothesis about contribution rather than an
established gap.

Every population/context, temporal, and geographic boundary has one of two forms:

- known value plus `user_input`, `source_artifact`, or `user_decision` basis;
- null value plus `unknown` status and `explicit_unknown` basis.

Assumptions, uncertainties, and decisions are separate. An assumption is not a user decision. An
unknown is not silently converted into either.

## Claim boundaries

Research framing may restate a user-observed phenomenon and explain why it could become a
research problem. It cannot establish prevalence, causality, a literature gap, uniqueness, or
novelty. A literature statement requires an evidence reference; omit it when evidence has not yet
been collected. No citation is better than a plausible citation.

## Depth boundary

The artifact should be deep enough to guide literature search but stop before committing to a
theory, hypotheses, sample size, sampling frame, operational measures, or analytic method. Record
such user-supplied choices as decisions; otherwise leave them for later capabilities.

## Completion judgment

Pass when all required slots are present, unknown scope is explicit, and no unsupported claim is
made. Do not block merely because important choices remain unknown. Recommend the single decision
that most reduces uncertainty next.
