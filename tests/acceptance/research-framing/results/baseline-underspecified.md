# Research Brief

## Working title

**Beyond Screen Time: Within-Person Pathways Linking TikTok Use to Affective Wellbeing Among U.S. Emerging Adults**

## Design assumptions

To make the study operational, this brief provisionally assumes:

- Population: U.S. adults aged 18–29 who use TikTok at least five days per week
- Period: October–December 2026
- Platform: TikTok
- Unit of analysis: use episodes and measurement occasions nested within individuals
- Theoretical angle: social-comparison and social-connectedness pathways within a person-specific media-effects framework

## Phenomenon and puzzle

TikTok use can expose the same individual to entertaining, socially supportive, aspirational, or distressing content within a single day. Consequently, similar amounts of use may coincide with either improved or diminished wellbeing. The central puzzle is why platform use appears beneficial in some moments and harmful in others, a question that aggregate screen-time measures cannot adequately answer.

## Research problem

Research framed around total time spent on social media risks treating heterogeneous experiences as a uniform exposure. It also frequently conflates stable differences between people with fluctuations occurring within the same person. A more precise account must identify the interactional pathways through which particular modes of TikTok use relate to momentary wellbeing while addressing temporal ordering and individual susceptibility.

## Primary research question

How do within-person changes in active relational use and passive content consumption on TikTok predict momentary affective wellbeing among U.S. emerging adults, and to what extent are these relationships explained by perceived social connectedness and upward social comparison?

## Supporting hypotheses

- **H1:** Passive consumption exceeding an individual’s usual level will predict increased negative affect and reduced positive affect at the subsequent measurement occasion.
- **H2:** The association between passive consumption and poorer affective wellbeing will be mediated by upward social comparison.
- **H3:** Active relational use exceeding an individual’s usual level will predict greater positive affect through perceived social connectedness.
- **H4:** These within-person relationships will vary across individuals, with stronger adverse comparison pathways among participants reporting lower baseline self-esteem.

## Communication/sociology conversation

The study contributes to debates about whether social-media effects should be conceptualized as uniform exposure effects or as contingent, person-specific processes. It connects platform-use research with scholarship on mediated social comparison, relational communication, and heterogeneous media effects. The named theoretical positioning remains provisional until a systematic literature review confirms the most defensible model and the study’s precise novelty.

## Unit and level of analysis

The primary unit is the **measurement occasion**, nested within the **individual user**. The focal level is therefore within-person audience experience, while between-person characteristics are modeled as moderators rather than treated as direct substitutes for episode-level processes.

## Scope and empirical material

A provisional sample of 600 English-speaking U.S. TikTok users aged 18–29 would complete:

1. A baseline survey measuring demographics, self-esteem, depressive symptoms, habitual platform use, and relevant dispositions.
2. A 14-day experience-sampling study with five randomized prompts per day.
3. At each prompt, brief measures of recent TikTok activity, active versus passive use, upward comparison, connectedness, positive affect, negative affect, and salient offline events.
4. Daily device screen-time verification for TikTok, collected without capturing private messages or viewed content.

The resulting observations would be analyzed using multilevel models and multilevel mediation, separating within-person deviations from between-person differences. Lagged specifications would improve temporal ordering, although they would not by themselves establish causality. The sample size requires simulation-based power analysis before preregistration.

## Provisional contribution

The study would provisionally shift the explanatory focus from whether TikTok use affects wellbeing to **when, through which social processes, and for whom particular use episodes matter**. Its theoretical contribution would be a pathway-dependent account distinguishing comparison-based and connection-based mechanisms. Its methodological contribution would be the separation of within-person dynamics from stable differences between users.

## Plausible alternative explanations

- Poor mood may lead to passive use rather than result from it.
- Offline stress, sleep, loneliness, or concurrent use of other platforms may influence both use and wellbeing.
- Content valence and algorithmic recommendations may explain associations attributed to behavioral mode.
- Self-reports may misclassify active and passive use.
- Attrition and prompt nonresponse may systematically exclude periods of especially poor wellbeing.

## Feasibility and ethical risks

Recruitment and experience sampling are feasible, but participant burden may reduce compliance. Device logs require strict data minimization, encryption, limited retention, and explicit consent. No message content, usernames, contact networks, or browsing histories should be collected. Because the study concerns mental wellbeing, the protocol should include distress resources and procedures for handling severe symptom disclosures. Institutional ethics approval is required.
