# Novelty comparison framework

## Unit of comparison

Compare the proposed contribution—not merely its topic words—with the closest inspected work. A
candidate may differ in phenomenon, context, construct, relation, mechanism, theory, evidence,
method, or level of analysis. Use only dimensions that materially change the knowledge claim.

For each row, state the candidate position and nearest-work position symmetrically. Avoid favorable
detail for the candidate and vague summaries for prior work. Preserve overlaps as carefully as
differences.

## Evidence boundary

An evidence reference combines a registered `source_id` with a passage, page, section, or explicit
fixture locator, such as `source-1#findings`. Metadata confirms identity, not what the work argues.
Candidate, retrieved, screened, and metadata-only records may identify what must be checked but
cannot establish a field-wide novelty claim.

## Contribution classification

Choose the best-fitting classification: theoretical, empirical, methodological, conceptual, or
integrative. Classification describes where the contribution would lie if supported; it is not an
independent quality score and does not prove novelty.

## Revision

When the claim fails, preserve the useful research idea by narrowing the population, phenomenon,
relation, mechanism, scope condition, or contribution type only when the evidence identifies that
boundary. Do not manufacture a narrower “gap” without support.
