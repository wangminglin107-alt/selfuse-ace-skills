# Baseline transcript: normal

## Research brief

### Working title

**Leaving the Upload Cycle: Burnout, Obligation, and Creator Exit Narratives on English-Language YouTube, 2023–2026**

### Status of the motivating observation

The claim that English-language creators increasingly published “exit videos” during 2023–2026 should be treated as a motivating observation, not an established trend. The study can examine how selected creators explain withdrawal without initially claiming that exit videos became more prevalent, that burnout objectively increased, or that YouTube caused creators to leave.

### Research problem

Creator exit videos are unusual moments of public accounting. Creators explain an interruption or end to work while addressing audiences through the platform from which they are withdrawing. These videos may reveal how creators understand burnout, responsibility, success, and autonomy within platform-dependent cultural production.

“Exit,” however, is not self-evident. It may mean permanent departure, an indefinite hiatus, reduced publishing, a change in format, or migration elsewhere. Likewise, obligations may be attributed not only to YouTube but also to audiences, sponsors, employees, personal identity, financial needs, or creators’ own expectations. The study should investigate these relations rather than assume a single platform-imposed obligation.

### Aim

To examine how English-language YouTube creators narratively and audiovisually construct withdrawal from regular content production, with particular attention to burnout and perceived obligations involving the platform.

### Research questions

1. How do creators narrate the trajectory from sustained production to withdrawal, reduction, or exit?
2. How is “burnout” named, described, performed, or contested in these accounts?
3. What obligations do creators invoke, and to whom or what are those obligations directed?
4. How do creators represent platform mechanisms—such as metrics, recommendation systems, monetization, and publishing regularity—as enabling, constraining, or irrelevant?
5. How do these narratives vary across exit type, channel scale, content genre, revenue dependence, and publication date?

The fifth question is comparative and exploratory; the corpus may not support strong subgroup claims.

### Conceptual orientation

The project could bring platform labor research into dialogue with work on creator culture, relational labor, algorithmic governance, and occupational burnout. These are provisional lenses rather than conclusions.

Key working concepts require empirical refinement:

- **Exit video:** a creator-authored video whose primary purpose is to announce or explain a substantial reduction, indefinite hiatus, platform migration, or cessation of regular YouTube production.
- **Burnout discourse:** explicit use of “burnout” or descriptions of exhaustion, detachment, diminished efficacy, anxiety, or unsustainable work. This is a discourse category, not a clinical diagnosis.
- **Platform obligation:** a narrated expectation to act in ways associated with maintaining visibility, revenue, or channel viability. Audience, sponsor, organizational, and self-imposed obligations should be coded separately.
- **Exit:** an announced orientation at the time of publication, not necessarily the creator’s eventual behavior.

### Scope and corpus

**Temporal boundary:** Videos published from January 1, 2023, through a declared 2026 corpus-freeze date.

**Language boundary:** Videos primarily spoken in English. This identifies a linguistic corpus, not a nationality or cultural region.

**Unit of analysis:** The individual exit video, supplemented by channel-level metadata needed to interpret it.

**Include videos that:**

- are published by the creator on their own channel;
- centrally announce or explain withdrawal, hiatus, major reduction, or departure;
- discuss burnout, unsustainable production, or obligations connected to continued creation;
- remain sufficiently accessible for transcription and audiovisual analysis.

**Exclude:**

- commentary about another creator’s departure;
- routine breaks with no account of production pressure;
- videos focused only on moving to another channel unless they explain relevant obligations;
- comments and audience reactions in the initial study;
- inferred burnout unsupported by the creator’s own account.

A purposive, maximum-variation sample of approximately 25–40 videos would be reasonable for an initial qualitative study. The final size should follow interpretive sufficiency and corpus diversity rather than a predetermined claim of representativeness.

### Discovery and sampling procedure

Use several documented search routes: predefined YouTube queries, web search, relevant creator-industry reporting, and snowballing from identified videos. Record query wording, date, account state, result position, and inclusion decision. Search results should not be treated as a neutral population because ranking is personalized, dynamic, and likely to privilege prominent channels.

Construct a sampling matrix covering:

- permanent exit, hiatus, reduced schedule, and migration;
- channel size bands;
- content genres;
- solo creators and production teams;
- apparent dependence on YouTube income;
- publication year.

Sampling categories should be revised if the videos resist these distinctions.

### Method

A qualitative multimodal narrative and discourse analysis is the strongest initial design.

1. Produce timestamped transcripts and manually correct passages central to the analysis.
2. Segment each video by narrative function: announcement, retrospective account, causal explanation, justification, audience address, and future orientation.
3. Code both speech and audiovisual presentation, including editing, setting, emotion displays, screenshots of metrics, and changes from the channel’s usual style.
4. Develop codes for:
   - meanings and symptoms of burnout;
   - production tempo and temporal pressure;
   - platform metrics and algorithmic imaginaries;
   - monetization and financial dependence;
   - obligations to audiences, sponsors, staff, platform, and self;
   - responsibility, guilt, gratitude, and failure;
   - coping, resistance, and alternative futures.
5. Compare cases across the sampling matrix while actively seeking negative cases—for example, creators who reject platform explanations or describe departure as opportunity rather than exhaustion.
6. Have a second researcher independently code a strategically varied subset to expose ambiguous definitions and revise the codebook. Agreement statistics are optional; interpretive disagreements should be documented.

### Claim–evidence boundaries

The video corpus could support claims about:

- how sampled creators publicly explain exit;
- recurring narrative and moral vocabularies;
- represented—not independently verified—platform pressures;
- variation within the assembled corpus.

It could not, by itself, establish:

- the prevalence of burnout among YouTube creators;
- whether exit videos increased between 2023 and 2026;
- the causal effects of YouTube’s systems;
- creators’ private motives or clinical conditions;
- how audiences interpreted the videos.

Those claims would require additional data such as longitudinal counts, interviews, platform data, or comment analysis.

### Provisional contribution

If supported, the study may show that exit videos function as public negotiations of platformed obligation: creators use withdrawal announcements to redraw boundaries among entrepreneurial autonomy, audience responsibility, and infrastructural dependence. A more modest alternative contribution would be a typology of exit narratives and the obligations invoked within them. It is also possible that platform obligation will not organize the corpus consistently; financial precarity, life transitions, health, or changing creative identity may prove more explanatory.

### Ethics

Although the videos are public, publication does not eliminate ethical risk. The study should avoid clinical diagnosis, distinguish creator statements from researcher inference, and consider whether naming smaller creators adds analytical value. Quotation and screenshots should be limited to what is necessary, with attention to context collapse and searchability. Corpus preservation, transcription, and quotation practices should be reviewed against platform terms, copyright rules, and institutional ethics requirements.

### Principal uncertainties

- Whether “exit video” is coherent enough to delimit a corpus.
- Whether enough videos explicitly connect departure to platform-related obligations.
- Whether search procedures can recover less visible and subsequently deleted videos.
- Whether English-language cases are comparable across national and occupational contexts.
- Whether retrospective accounts are strategic performances, candid explanations, or both.
- Whether the 2026 corpus represents a complete year; the freeze date must be stated.
- Whether the project should analyze burnout as a creator category, a scholarly construct, or both.

**Decision gate:** Proceed to full sampling only if the pilot yields a coherent analytical object and sufficient variation.
